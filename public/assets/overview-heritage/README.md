# Macro overview: collection photographs

The two JPEGs are unchanged `primaryImageSmall` (web-large) files obtained from
The Metropolitan Museum of Art's official Collection API on 2026-10-16. The
complete object records, acquisition URLs, file sizes and SHA-256 hashes are
archived beside them. Both records state `isPublicDomain: true`; both public
catalogue pages display **Public Domain**.

The Met's [Open Access policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access)
releases images of public-domain works and basic collection data under CC0.

| `met-manuscript-cover-37610.jpg` | Manuscript Cover with the Buddha Shakyamuni, Attended by Manjushri and Vajrapani, 1987.407.6 | About early 12th century | https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/37610 |

These are pictures of collection objects, not photographs of the narrated
1042 journey, current temple holdings, or objects placed at a known historical
workshop. The museum attributes both to Tibet but does not locate their making
at Tholing; the overview does not invent that link.

The UI's observation rectangles are ordinary reading aids over visible features.
The portrait's teaching gesture and palm-leaf manuscript come from the museum
description; the cover's rectangle marks the visible wooden object. Rectangles
do not modify the source pixels, measure an original object, decode an
inscription, or claim expert survey authority.

The National Museum of China's Tang–Tubo stele rubbing remains a linked
catalogue source. No rubbing image is copied because an appropriate open image
licence was not established. The overview does not fabricate a rubbing image.

`overviewHeritagePresentation` selects the short picture windows from literal
tokens in the exact existing recordings' text. Source word timing drives the
text progress, and the picture disappears when the narration returns to the
next larger subject. There is no independent animation clock or arbitrary
recording seek.
